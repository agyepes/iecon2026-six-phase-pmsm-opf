Stator copper losses

Cycle-average winding losses in watts, as in the OPF paper’s Fig. 8 [25]. The values below are total losses in the five healthy stator phases; phase a is open and contributes zero. Healthy-operation losses are not computed.

PCu=SCL=Rs∑k=26ik,rms2=RsJSCL

Rs = 1.4 Ω per phase. In the one-open-phase-fault paper [25], JSCL = SCL / Rs is the loss objective normalized by resistance, with units A². The figures and this viewer show SCL in W.

Calculation and interpretation

For the proposed Fourier current references, orthogonality gives the expression in Eq. (9) of the one-open-phase-fault paper [25]:

JSCL=12∑k=26∑h[(Ik,hRe)2+(Ik,hIm)2]

k = 2…6 denotes phases b…f; h spans the retained odd harmonics 1, 3, …, 21. The other methods use Rs times the cycle average of the summed squared phase currents on the selected angular grid. The proposed OPF optimization first minimizes torque ripple and then minimizes SCL within its near-minimum-ripple constraint and the drive limits [25].

A small SCL value from a method exceeding voltage or current limits is not an achievable saving at that point. No NSBE-FRML loss is reported when its requested mean torque is unavailable. Italic Atallah values flag an instantaneous-torque check failure. The healthy case is represented only by its torque–speed boundary.

How to read this plot

Line colour identifies one method: the proposed method [25], NSBE-FRML [11], or the Atallah method [13], evaluated with γ=1, 2.3 or 3 as in the one-open-phase-fault paper [25]. Each line style applies only to the method with that colour under the fixed 1% limit allowance.

A solid segment means that both sampled endpoints meet that method’s checks within the 1% tolerance. A dotted segment means that at least one of its two sampled endpoints fails a limit or another requirement, including the applicable torque-reference criterion. SCL itself has no pass/fail threshold.

NSBE-FRML is checked against the requested mean torque after adjusting its internal reference. Atallah retains its instantaneous-torque check. Curves are omitted when the applicable torque target cannot be achieved. Use of the NSBE-FRML maximizing branch alone does not hide a curve or mark it as a torque-target failure.

Speeds are sampled every 20 r/min, with the first LUT column and original proposed boundary also included. Lines connect samples and do not prove feasibility between them. The proposed curve stops at its original reference envelope; NSBE-FRML has no curve for an unavailable mean-torque target; Atallah curves stop wherever their current references are unavailable or their instantaneous-torque check fails.

Back-EMF definition, harmonics and source

The voltage waveforms use ek = ωm e′k, with mechanical angular speed ωm = 2πn/60 in rad/s and n in r/min. The speed-normalized quantity e′k has units V/(rad/s). Electrical angle spans 0–360°; the six phase axes are displaced by 60°.

These are the back-EMF waveforms used in the simulation calculations, obtained from the original backEMF.mat and angleEMF.mat data using linear interpolation and half-wave symmetrization, as in the MATLAB functions used to evaluate the comparison methods in the paper’s simulations. The proposed and comparison models use the same waveforms after aligning their angular grids. The 1100 r/min option uses the speed of Fig. 4 in the one-open-phase-fault paper [25]; small differences from its original input traces can result from this symmetrization and angular sampling. The current LUT’s H = 21 setting does not truncate these back-EMF data.

The harmonic view uses a discrete Fourier transform of the same uniformly sampled electrical period. The six phases are assumed to share the same harmonic amplitudes, so phase a represents all six. One bar per harmonic shows the sinusoidal peak amplitude, rather than the RMS value, at odd electrical harmonic orders 1–13. The vertical scale can show amplitudes or percentages of the fundamental: 100 times each harmonic amplitude divided by the fundamental amplitude. These ratios use the speed-normalized machine spectrum and are independent of speed. Order 1 is the fundamental (100%); its bar can be excluded to enlarge the higher harmonics without changing the percentage reference. Other orders are omitted from this view. This display selection does not truncate the back-EMF waveforms used by the calculations.

Opening phase a imposes ia = 0, while retaining ea. Hover or tap to inspect waveform samples or harmonic amplitudes. Waveform export includes all six phases; harmonic export includes the representative phase a. Both use the selected display scale.

Model, data sources and interpretation

Shared machine and limits

Machine parameters and nominal limits follow the one-open-phase-fault simulations in [25].

  • Symmetrical six-phase PMSM, star connection, one neutral point; phase a open; five pole pairs
  • Phase resistance 1.4 Ω; VSD inductances: 12, 12, 11.3, 11.3, 9.4 and 9.4 mH
  • Peak phase current ≤ 4.34 A; peak voltage between healthy phases ≤ 290 V; peak-to-peak torque ripple ≤ 2 N·m
  • Normalized back-EMF in V/(mechanical rad/s), linearly interpolated and half-wave symmetrized; cogging torque is zero
  • Electrical frequency = speed/12 Hz. Voltages use actual speed, including below the first retained LUT frequency

Proposed current references

The one-open-phase-fault current-reference formulation is described in [25].

Odd harmonics 1, 3, …, 21. The original H=21, nt=250 efficient LUT is completed by holding the last feasible coefficient vector in each torque row’s high-speed tail (429 cells). Existing feasible coefficients and the original boundaries are preserved. This adapts the completion approach from the LUT-reduction paper [26] to the OPF grid. The reduction paper demonstrates a healthy-case LUT; this file evaluates the completed compact OPF grid independently.

Coefficients are interpolated linearly in torque and frequency. Below 79 Hz, the first retained coefficient column is held. Small extrapolation above the compact frequency axis is permitted only inside the original full LUT reference envelope. No torque-ratio scaling or additional torque-sign factor is applied. Torque, losses and constraints are recomputed from the resulting currents.

Healthy reference

The exact stored Tref_array and f1High from the healthy H=9, nt=150 LUT define this boundary. The LUT method is described in the healthy-case paper [19]. No healthy current coefficients are included or recalculated.

Constraint checks for each method

Each table row and loss-curve segment is assessed for its own method using 250 electrical-angle samples. The checks cover peak current, peak voltage between healthy phases, peak-to-peak torque ripple, the applicable torque criterion, the zero sum of healthy-phase currents, and zero current in the open phase a. Stator copper loss is compared without a pass/fail limit.

“Constraints met at checked angles” means the nominal limits and torque/current conditions pass within numerical tolerance. “Within 1% allowance” means a result passes after applying the allowed deviations below. Both statuses satisfy this viewer’s selected criterion.

The fixed 1% limit allowance permits a peak current of 4.3834 A and peak-to-peak torque ripple of 2.02 N·m, and applies a 1% tolerance above the specified peak line-voltage limit.

Numerical tolerances are 0.0001 A for the proposed peak-current and neutral/open-phase checks, 0.001 V for its voltage check, and 0.0001 N·m for the torque-ripple check of all methods. NSBE-FRML and Atallah retain their saved MATLAB current, voltage and neutral/open-phase tolerances. The 1% allowance does not increase either direct method’s torque-error tolerance.

Comparison methods and curves

NSBE-FRML [11] and the Atallah et al. (2003) [13] variants with γ=1, 2.3 and 3 use the current-selection calculations, periodic flux integration, central current derivative and settings from the MATLAB functions used to evaluate the comparison methods in the paper’s simulations [25]. Here, γ scales the flux-weakening weighting factor [14]; it is fixed for each comparison variant. Both direct current generators use the nominal 4.34 A peak phase-current limits. The Atallah variants retain the entered instantaneous-torque target.

NSBE-FRML mean torque in this version. The internal torque reference for NSBE-FRML [11] is adjusted to produce the entered cycle-mean torque. It may exceed the entered mean torque when the torque-maximizing branch is used. The table reports the internal reference and achieved mean torque; the maximizing-branch fraction is informational and does not itself indicate failure. The adjustment was verified against the resulting torque waveforms at 250 electrical-angle samples, with independent calculations of mean torque and copper loss.

What this viewer evaluates. The star-connection current constraint and NSBE-FRML torque-maximizing branch are included. The ripple-control and RMS-current limitation stages of the complete NSBE-FRML method in [11] are not implemented.

All displayed boundaries use the nominal 290 V line-voltage limit, without the 1% voltage allowance used for operating-point checks. The proposed and healthy boundaries use the speed limits and sampling stored in their LUTs. For each torque value, each Atallah boundary shows the upper end of the first consecutive range of speeds that passes that method’s checks, following the selection used in Fig. 7 of the one-open-phase-fault paper [25]. If the calculation becomes feasible again at higher speeds after a failing range, that later feasible range is omitted from the boundary curve. Each entered operating point is still evaluated independently of the displayed boundaries. The NSBE-FRML boundary is recalculated using the adjusted current references and the 290 V limit at the 250 checked angles; its existing 1% current and ripple allowances and numerical tolerances are retained. This viewer covers positive torque and the zero-torque limit.

The NSBE-FRML curve in Fig. 7 of the IEEE IECON 2026 one-open-phase-fault paper [25] requires the requested instantaneous torque to be achieved at every sampled angle. The adjusted mean-torque criterion and retained current/ripple allowances change the NSBE-FRML boundary, even though this version uses the same nominal voltage limit as the paper.

This file evaluates the steady-state mathematical model. It contains no FEA results or closed-loop transient simulation.

Reminder of advantages of NSBE-FRML [11] over Atallah [13]

These advantages refer to the complete NSBE-FRML method in [11]. See What this viewer evaluates for the features included in this HTML.

  • Suitability for star connection. NSBE-FRML explicitly enforces a zero instantaneous sum of the healthy-phase currents, as required by a star connection with an isolated neutral. The original Atallah formulation uses independently supplied phases and does not include this neutral-current constraint. The Atallah variants evaluated here are adapted to enforce it. [11], Sections II and IV; [13], Sections IV and V.
  • Torque-maximizing branch within peak-current limits. If the instantaneous torque reference supplied to the current generator cannot be achieved at an electrical angle within the peak phase-current limits, NSBE-FRML generates currents that maximize the achievable instantaneous torque at that angle while respecting those limits and the neutral condition. This minimizes the instantaneous torque shortfall relative to that reference, although the resulting torque may have ripple. [11], Section IV-C and Fig. 8.
  • Additional mean torque with a specified ripple limit. The complete NSBE-FRML method combines the maximizing calculation with torque-reference saturation to exploit an allowed ripple margin and increase mean torque beyond the ripple-free range. A limitation of Atallah's formulation is that it targets the requested instantaneous torque without exploiting an allowed ripple margin to increase mean torque. [11], Section V and Fig. 10; [13], Section IV.
  • Automatic gradual RMS-current reduction. The complete method monitors each phase-current RMS. If any phase exceeds its RMS rating, it progressively reduces the torque reference until all phase RMS currents respect their ratings. This allows a gradual transition from transient overload to steady-state operation within the RMS limits. The original Atallah paper does not describe this RMS-limitation stage. [11], Section VI and Fig. 14; [13].
