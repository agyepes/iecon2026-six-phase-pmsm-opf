7. WHY THE OVERSHOOTS ARE REAL
-----------------------------
The 250-angle original ramp has peak maxima about
4.340000218 A and 290.000123650 V. Finer and continuously enclosed waveforms
show the larger maxima in Section 2. They are intersample peaks of the same
unchanged current-reference coefficients and back-EMF recipe.

The support vertices themselves already show continuous-angle violations:
168 vertices
exceed the current limit and 579
exceed the voltage limit. One support vertex, T*=5.7 Nm at 1500 r/min, has
continuous torque ripple about 2.005145735 Nm, although its 250-angle ripple
is below 2 Nm. The selected interpolated trajectory still passes ripple.
Valid stored support is not a continuous-angle feasibility certificate.

If double optimizer outputs were rounded to nearest float32 by MATLAB single(),
the conditional maximum storage uncertainty over the support is only
3.71671120055146e-07 A and
1.76059600582921e-05 V.
The original double solver outputs are unavailable, so this conditional bound
does not assign a precise cause to every tiny 250-sample violation. It does
show that storage quantization cannot explain the much larger dense peaks.

Voltage interpolation has a separate issue: actual speed multiplies the
derivative of interpolated current coefficients. Thus bilinear coefficients
do not make voltage a convex interpolation of vertex voltages evaluated at
different vertex speeds. This audit accounts for the exact quadratic-speed
dependence using six Bernstein control waveforms per cell.

At the worst original 250-angle voltage point (source index
1,172,807, 1427.389033766698 r/min,
T*=5.472178489238 Nm, pair ce),
the absolute convex combination of vertex voltages at the same angle is
290.000001146930 V.
Actual speed coupled to the interpolated current derivative increases this
by 0.000122503141 V,
giving 290.000123650070 V. The exact
decomposition agrees across all ten lines within
9.25685851756175e-14 V.
This identifies the interpolation contribution to that tiny sampled peak;
it does not recover or infer the historical double optimizer outputs.

The current generator source tests tau_min<=2, then sets the final QP bound
to tau_min+0.01 without explicitly capping it at 2. A regenerated version
should use min(tau_min+0.01,2). This is a source-code issue identified by direct
inspection, not a claim about unavailable historical H21 solver logs.
